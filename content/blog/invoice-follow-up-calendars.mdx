# Invoice Follow-up Calendars

Show reference, last contact, next date, responsible person, and escalation condition. The best support keeps the record useful without quietly turning preparation into an unauthorized decision.

## Invoice Follow-up Calendars: define the record before the work starts

Write down the source of truth, required fields, expected handoff, review owner, and stop condition. For invoice follow-up dates and ownership organized, include the latest source date, status vocabulary, and evidence needed to resolve an exception. This gives an assistant a stable reference and gives the owner a fair way to review the result.

## Separate preparation from judgment

The assistant can collect, compare, format, and flag. The owner should retain approvals, policy interpretation, sensitive decisions, and irreversible changes. Keep an explicit "needs review" status rather than filling a gap with an assumption. The [service role library](/services) helps compare adjacent support responsibilities, while the [provider vetting checklist](/provider-vetting) gives buyers questions about access and review.

## Make the handoff useful next week

Record what changed, what is waiting, what evidence is missing, and who takes the next action. Review a small sample early, note recurring corrections, and update the brief when the same question appears twice. Keep access limited to the systems needed for this task lane. The [NIST small business cybersecurity guidance](https://www.nist.gov/itl/smallbusinesscyber) provides a practical baseline for protecting business information.

## Questions to settle before delegation

### What should be supplied first?

Provide the approved source, a completed example, the required format, deadline, and named reviewer. Also state which requests must stop for owner approval.

### When should the work be escalated?

Escalate when information conflicts, a sensitive decision is involved, the request falls outside the brief, or the expected result cannot be checked from available evidence.
