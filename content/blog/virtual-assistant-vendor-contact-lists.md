# Virtual Assistant Vendor Contact Lists

Vendor contact lists are operational records, not address books. They should show who handles which question, when the information was last checked, and what evidence supports the entry.

## Virtual Assistant Vendor Contact Lists: define the record

Include the vendor name, contact role, channel, scope of responsibility, verification date, and source. A role is often more durable than a personal name, so store both when appropriate.

## Distinguish primary and backup contacts

Mark the normal route and the escalation route separately. Do not label an unverified person as a backup merely because their name appears in an old message.

## Review by trigger

Check contacts after a change notice, failed delivery, renewal conversation, or ownership transfer. The [contact maintenance guide](/blog/virtual-assistant-vendor-contact-maintenance) explains review points, while the [vendor onboarding guide](/blog/virtual-assistant-vendor-onboarding) covers first capture.

## Keep outreach bounded

Prepare the message and source details, then follow the owner’s authority rules before sending. Do not disclose unrelated client information while requesting a routine update.

## Protect contact data

Restrict editing rights and retain only fields that serve the relationship. Consult [NIST small business cybersecurity guidance](https://www.nist.gov/itl/smallbusinesscyber) for practical access principles.
