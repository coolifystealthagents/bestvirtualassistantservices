# Virtual Assistant Knowledge Base Updates

For knowledge base updates, begin with the record that makes the next decision possible, then keep the owner and stopping point visible.

## Virtual Assistant Knowledge Base Updates: Start with the reader’s failed question

A knowledge-base update should begin with the question a customer or colleague could not answer. Record the product area, audience, and point of confusion. This keeps the article grounded in a real information need instead of becoming a general rewrite.

## Check the source before editing

Compare the proposed answer with the current product behavior, approved policy, and support history. Mark claims that need an owner’s confirmation. If the source is uncertain, publish the question or route it for review rather than filling the gap with plausible language.

## Write for scanning

Use a direct answer first, then prerequisites, numbered steps, exceptions, and a clear next action. Explain unfamiliar terms at their first appearance. Headings should reflect the reader’s questions, not the department that owns the material.

## Make change visible

Record what changed, why it changed, and which older instruction is no longer valid. If the article replaces another page, link or redirect deliberately. A visible revision note helps support staff understand why a familiar answer has shifted.

## Give the page an owner

Assign a review date and a person who can verify the source. Watch search terms that lead readers to the page and questions that still produce support contacts. The [Nielsen Norman Group overview of knowledge management](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/knowledge-management/) reinforces the value of keeping organizational knowledge findable and current.


## Keep the decision visible

For this topic, preserve the source and route uncertainty to the named owner. Reference: [Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/knowledge-management/).
