# Virtual Assistant Call Preparation Briefs

A call brief should help a busy participant enter a conversation with the right context, questions, and boundaries. Its job is to reduce searching, not to predict what another person will say.

## Virtual Assistant Call Preparation Briefs: identify the decision

Write the purpose of the call in one sentence and name the decision or result expected. If no decision is expected, state whether the call is for discovery, status, or relationship maintenance.

## Separate facts from prompts

Use a short facts section for confirmed information and a separate questions section for items to explore. Label information that needs checking. This gives the owner a reliable starting point without hiding uncertainty.

## Match detail to the audience

An executive brief may need three decisions and two risks. A specialist may need field definitions and prior correspondence. Keep supporting material linked rather than copying every message into the brief.

## Make ownership explicit

Mark who opens the call, who answers each question, and who records the follow-up. The [meeting preparation process](/blog/virtual-assistant-meeting-preparation-process) helps with repeatable setup, while the [meeting agenda guide](/blog/virtual-assistant-meeting-agenda-operations) helps when several people share the discussion.

## Close the loop

After the call, compare the brief with the actual commitments and capture only confirmed next steps. Protect sensitive notes using [NIST small business cybersecurity guidance](https://www.nist.gov/itl/smallbusinesscyber), especially when briefs travel across systems.
