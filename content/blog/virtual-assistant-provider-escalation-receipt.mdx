# Capture a Provider Escalation Receipt Before Closing a Case

Published October 22, 2026.

## Capture a Provider Escalation Receipt Before Closing a Case: Define the delegated lane

During define the delegated lane, Capture a Provider Escalation Receipt Before Closing a Case treats provider escalation receipts as a bounded operating lane for clients receiving escalated assistant work, not an implied transfer of client authority. Checkpoint 1 records escalation ID, reason, context packet, accepting owner, acknowledgement, and resolution under BVAS22-B-virtual-assistant-provider-escalation-receipt-1. The assistant may collect, compare, prepare, and route within approved instructions; the service owner retains commitments, money movement, account changes, privacy or legal interpretation, policy exceptions, and release decisions. A status label without its source, timestamp, and acceptance evidence does not prove the work is ready.

For define the delegated lane, the reviewer exercises accepted, rejected, rerouted, timed out, and reopened escalations and preserves the original request, authoritative source, permitted action, observed result, expected result, stop reason, and named recipient. Particular attention goes to a case marked closed after a transfer that no owner accepted. If the written rule does not resolve the condition, the assistant holds the item safely, keeps uncertainty visible, and escalates without inventing permission. This checkpoint succeeds when another operator can reconstruct the chronology and repeat the completion check.

The define the delegated lane record distinguishes active handling from time waiting on a client owner, customer, vendor, system, or source. It also preserves rejected, reopened, corrected, and abandoned cases so a dashboard cannot reward premature closure. For provider escalation receipts, managers should review both ordinary and consequential examples, verify the user-visible or recipient-visible outcome, and name the condition that triggers retraining, tighter scope, added approval capacity, or suspension. Expansion requires a new access and authority decision rather than unused provider hours.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can the provider approve an exception?

Only when the client has granted that exact authority in a current written rule. Otherwise the provider records and routes the exception.

### When is the workflow complete?

Completion requires the authoritative result, acceptance evidence, outward communication where needed, and reconciliation of temporary access or duplicate artifacts.
