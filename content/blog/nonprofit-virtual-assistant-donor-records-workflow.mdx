# Scope a Virtual Assistant for Nonprofit Donor Records

Published 2026-10-05.

Donor administration combines data quality, stewardship, finance reconciliation, privacy, and decisions about recognition. A virtual assistant can maintain records and prepare approved acknowledgements, but fundraising and finance owners must define what a gift means, how restrictions are recorded, and who corrects a tax or accounting issue.

## Scope a Virtual Assistant for Nonprofit Donor Records: Reconcile gifts before thanking donors

Match processor, bank, campaign, and CRM identifiers without treating an unmatched deposit as a completed donor record. Preserve the source and route conflicts to finance.

## Control household and organization identities

Set rules for duplicates, joint recognition, soft credits, and organizational contacts. An assistant flags uncertain matches rather than merging history on name similarity.

## Generate acknowledgements from approved facts

Use authorized templates and verified gift fields. Keep tax characterization, valuation of noncash gifts, and legal statements with qualified owners.

## Honor communication and recognition preferences

Record anonymity, public-name, channel, and opt-out choices in the authoritative system. Review exports and event lists before disclosure.

## Separate restricted-gift decisions

The assistant can capture donor wording and attach documents, while the fundraising and finance owners decide acceptance, restriction, and coding.

## Audit a sample end to end

Trace selected gifts from source through reconciliation, CRM, acknowledgement, campaign reporting, and preference handling, including corrections.

## The restricted-check conflict should remain visible

A check arrives with a note naming a program, while the campaign form shows unrestricted support. The assistant scans the note to the controlled record, holds the acknowledgement, and routes the discrepancy to fundraising and finance. After their documented decision, the assistant applies the code and approved wording, then confirms the public recognition preference. No one rewrites the donor's note or guesses a restriction to clear the queue.

Use a check memo that conflicts with the campaign form. Fundraising and finance decide restriction and coding; the assistant retains both sources and pauses public or tax language until that decision is recorded.

## Reconcile settlement evidence before generating thanks

Reconcile a processor settlement against individual gifts before any acknowledgement is released. Timing differences and fees should remain visible instead of being forced into a balanced donor total.

Follow three gifts with different failure risks. For an online gift, compare the processor event, settlement, fee, CRM entry, acknowledgement, and campaign report; a timing difference remains an explained reconciling item until the deposit arrives. For a mailed check, retain its intake context, memo, deposit reference, and any campaign form expressing a different purpose. Fundraising and finance decide what the discrepancy means before acknowledgement wording is released. For a noncash item, record description and receipt while authorized owners retain acceptance and valuation statements. Then test identity and preference controls. Similar names, a household address, or a shared employer do not justify a merge. The reviewer should see the evidence used, retained source fields, correction history, and opt-out treatment. Generate both an internal reconciliation report and a public recognition list for an anonymous donor. Necessary internal detail stays under restricted access while the public export suppresses the name. Finally, correct one amount or designation and trace the correction through every downstream artifact. If the provider fixes only the CRM screen while an acknowledgement, campaign total, or event list stays wrong, the workflow has not closed the error. This review tests money, intent, identity, communication, and disclosure as connected obligations rather than isolated data-entry steps. It also gives managers a concrete basis for deciding which exceptions require immediate review, which can wait for routine reconciliation, and what evidence an assistant must retain before work is considered complete.

## Test identity and anonymity in the same export cycle

Present two similar donor identities with different communication preferences. The assistant must hold the merge until evidence supports identity and must preserve the surviving provenance and opt-out history.

Mark a donor anonymous and then generate an event list. The test passes only if public recognition fields remain suppressed while authorized staff can still reconcile the underlying gift.

## Noncash gifts expose the limit of administrative coding

Add a noncash gift with no approved valuation. The record may capture description and receipt, but valuation claims and acceptance decisions stay with qualified owners.

## Trace one correction through every donor-facing artifact

Trace one corrected gift through settlement, CRM, acknowledgement, campaign reporting, and preference handling. Every downstream change needs a source, owner, and retained prior value.

Before approval, compare the batch total with bank evidence and inspect one acknowledgement chosen from each intake channel. Confirm that tax or recognition language came from an approved template, the donor's communication preference was applied at send time, and a held gift could not slip into an automated mailing while its restriction remained unresolved.

## Decide whether the record protects intent, not just totals

Record the queue states that prevent premature acknowledgement: awaiting settlement, identity uncertain, designation conflict, noncash review, and communication preference unresolved. Each state needs an owner and a release condition. This lets staff see why a gift is held without allowing the assistant to settle the underlying fundraising, accounting, or tax question.

The donor workflow should preserve intent from source evidence through reconciliation, acknowledgement, reporting, and recognition preferences. Expansion is appropriate only when restriction conflicts, identity uncertainty, noncash questions, and anonymity survive as owned exceptions instead of being cleaned away to speed receipt processing.
