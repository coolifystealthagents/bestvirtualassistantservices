## Control Priority Overrides in an Assistant Task Queue: Define the delegated lane

During define the delegated lane, Control Priority Overrides in an Assistant Task Queue treats assistant task priority overrides as a bounded operating lane for operations teams sharing a delegated queue, not an implied transfer of client authority. Checkpoint 1 records task ID, original priority, override reason, requester, approver, and reset date under BVAS22-B-virtual-assistant-priority-override-control-1. The assistant may collect, compare, prepare, and route within approved instructions; the queue owner retains commitments, money movement, account changes, privacy or legal interpretation, policy exceptions, and release decisions. A status label without its source, timestamp, and acceptance evidence does not prove the work is ready.

For define the delegated lane, the reviewer exercises routine priority, executive request, conflicting deadlines, and reset and preserves the original request, authoritative source, permitted action, observed result, expected result, stop reason, and named recipient. Particular attention goes to loud requests repeatedly displacing scheduled high-consequence work. It also preserves rejected, reopened, corrected, and abandoned cases so a dashboard cannot reward premature closure. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can the provider approve an exception?

Only when the client has granted that exact authority in a current written rule. Otherwise the provider records and routes the exception.

### When is the workflow complete?

Completion requires the authoritative result, acceptance evidence, outward communication where needed, and reconciliation of temporary access or duplicate artifacts.
