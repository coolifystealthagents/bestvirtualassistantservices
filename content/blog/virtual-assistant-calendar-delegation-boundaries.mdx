# Virtual Assistant Calendar Delegation Boundaries

For calendar delegation boundaries, begin with the record that makes the next decision possible, then keep the owner and stopping point visible.

## Virtual Assistant Calendar Delegation Boundaries: Map the calendar’s decision points

Calendar support becomes safer when access is described by decisions rather than by a vague request to manage everything. List what may be added, moved, or removed; identify invitations that need approval; and name the person who owns conflicts. This turns a shared calendar into a set of clear permissions instead of a collection of assumptions.

## Choose the narrowest useful access

Start with visibility and drafting rights, then expand only when the business case is clear. A person preparing meeting options may not need permission to send invitations or edit recurring events. Review access when responsibilities change, and remove it promptly when the work ends. Google’s calendar guidance explains the practical difference between viewing, editing, and sharing calendars.

## Protect holds and personal blocks

A private block can be useful without disclosing its reason. Record only the availability signal needed for scheduling. Treat travel time, health appointments, family commitments, and focus periods as protected unless the owner explicitly says otherwise. A clear boundary prevents an efficient-looking calendar from quietly eroding the owner’s priorities.

## Handle conflicts with a stated rule

When two requests collide, preserve both facts and apply the agreed ranking: client commitment, deadline, travel constraint, or another owner-defined priority. Do not select the more senior-looking attendee by instinct. Send the conflict with options and the consequence of each option so the decision-maker can answer quickly.

## Audit delegation occasionally

Once a month or after a role change, compare the access list with actual responsibilities. Check shared calendars, delegates, notification rules, and old recurring events. A short review catches permissions that outlived the original assignment. See [Google Calendar Help](https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/37082) for the platform’s sharing controls.


## Keep the decision visible

For this topic, preserve the source and route uncertainty to the named owner. Reference: [Google Calendar Help](https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/37082).
