# Virtual Assistant Lead Qualification Notes

Lead qualification notes should help the next person understand what was said, what was verified, and what still needs to be asked. They are not a hidden scoring system. A careful virtual assistant records the conversation in a way that respects the prospect’s words, separates facts from interpretation, and keeps sensitive information out of ordinary business notes.

## Virtual Assistant Lead Qualification Notes: Keep evidence and inference apart

Begin with direct statements. Use quotation marks sparingly and only when the wording matters, then identify the speaker and date. “We need help keeping weekday inquiries organized” is evidence of a stated need. “They are overwhelmed and ready to buy” is an inference unless the person said those things plainly.

Create separate fields for verified firmographic facts, open questions, constraints, timing, decision process, consent or contact preferences, and next action. A firmographic fact might be a company name or industry that the prospect confirmed. An open question might be the number of people involved in review. A constraint might be a preferred communication channel or a stated start window. These categories prevent a vague impression from becoming a permanent record.

## Capture the conversation in layers

The first layer is the prospect’s request in their own terms. The second is context that was confirmed during the exchange. The third is what remains unknown. The fourth is the agreed next step, including who will act and by when. If no next step was agreed, write that plainly rather than implying a meeting or follow-up permission.

Timing needs careful wording. “Asked about support after an internal planning meeting next month” is different from “plans to start next month.” Decision process also needs evidence. Record “said a partner will review the proposed scope” rather than “not the decision maker.” The latter may be true, but it is an unsupported conclusion if the conversation did not establish authority.

## Use an evidence-versus-inference test

For every sentence, ask whether another reader could point to the source of the claim. If yes, label it as stated or verified. If not, turn it into an open question or remove it. This test is especially important for tone, urgency, budget, health, identity, and personal circumstances. An assistant can document a constraint the prospect volunteered. It should not diagnose a reason, guess an emotional state, or infer a protected characteristic.

The same discipline applies to service fit. If the prospect describes a task that appears related to administrative support, record the task and the requested outcome. Do not promise that the request can be accepted until the responsible reviewer confirms scope. Neutral uncertainty is more useful than confident overreach.

## A flawed note and a careful rewrite

Consider this flawed note: “Hot lead, small company, owner is disorganized, needs help immediately, probably cannot decide, follow up next week.” It contains a temperature label with no method, a judgment about the owner, an unsupported assumption about company size, and a follow-up instruction that may not have been agreed.

A more defensible version would read: “Stated need: wants assistance organizing weekday inquiry follow-up. Verified: contact identified the business as a local design studio. Timing: asked whether an introductory conversation could happen during the week of September 14. Open question: who else reviews service scope was not discussed. Preference: requested email as the first contact. Next action: send the requested availability options after the contact confirms a day; no appointment is scheduled yet.”

The rewrite is not colder. It is more useful because it tells the next colleague what is known, what is not known, and what must not be assumed. It also avoids making the prospect repeat information.

## Handle consent and preferences precisely

Record communication preferences only when the prospect expressed them or when an approved system captured them through a clear choice. Distinguish “prefers email” from “consented to all future marketing.” Those are not interchangeable. If a person asks not to be contacted through a channel, preserve that request where the approved system allows it and avoid restating unnecessary personal details in the narrative.

Do not copy credentials, payment details, private customer records, health information, or unrelated personal history into a lead note. If a specialist needs protected context, use the approved secure location and link only according to access rules. The [Federal Trade Commission privacy and security guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/privacy-security) offers a useful reminder to limit collection and protect information that is retained.

## Make the handoff reversible

A good note lets a reviewer correct a misunderstanding without hunting through an impressionistic paragraph. Include the conversation date, source channel, and the specific record or message that supports the summary when the system permits. Mark corrections rather than silently rewriting history. If a statement is later disproved, retain the original as a dated report and add the updated fact.

Use plain labels such as stated, verified, open question, preference, constraint, and next action. Avoid labels such as ideal customer, poor fit, or high intent unless the company has a documented definition and the authorized reviewer applies it. A neutral vocabulary reduces the chance that a casual phrase will steer later judgment.

## Prepare a concise review handoff

Before sharing the note, check that the reader can answer six questions: What did the person ask for? What facts were confirmed? What remains unknown? What constraints or preferences were stated? What action was agreed? Who owns that action and when is it due? If any answer is missing, say “not discussed” or “not yet confirmed.”

## Let uncertainty remain visible

The strongest qualification note does not force a conclusion. It creates a reliable starting point for the person who will review the request next. By separating direct statements, verified facts, open questions, constraints, timing, decision process, preferences, and next action, a virtual assistant supports respectful service without covert scoring or unsupported promises.
