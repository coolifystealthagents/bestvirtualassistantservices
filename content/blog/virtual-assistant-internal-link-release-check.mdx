# Virtual Assistant Internal Link Release Check

Published September 1, 2026.

Internal links should help a reader continue a task, not merely increase the number of connections between pages.
Use the [content calendar workflow](/blog/virtual-assistant-content-calendar-workflow) to place this control in the daily sequence, the [editorial quality checklist](/blog/virtual-assistant-editorial-quality-checklist) for adjacent review steps, and [NIST small business cybersecurity guidance](https://www.nist.gov/itl/smallbusinesscyber) when the routine handles business information.

## Virtual Assistant Internal Link Release Check: operating steps

Label each proposed connection as definition, prerequisite, next step, evidence, or related workflow. Remove links that do not help the reader at that point in the article.

## Confirm destination identity

Open the final route and compare its title and topic with the anchor text. Watch for old slugs, redirect chains, and similar pages that answer a different question.

## Check placement and repetition

Put the link near the sentence it supports and use descriptive language. Avoid repeating the same destination throughout a short article unless separate sections genuinely require it.

## Retest after deployment packaging

Validate links in the production build because path handling can differ from an editor preview. Record broken, redirected, and inaccessible destinations separately for efficient correction.

## Frequently asked questions

### Must every article link to a service page?

No. Add a service link only when it answers the reader's next reasonable question.

### Should external links be part of this check?

They need their own source and availability review; do not treat internal and external destinations as identical risks.
