# Virtual Assistant Canonical URL Check for Article Publishing

Published September 1, 2026.

A canonical check prevents one article from acquiring several competing addresses during routine publishing.
Use the [content calendar workflow](/blog/virtual-assistant-content-calendar-workflow) to place this control in the daily sequence, the [editorial quality checklist](/blog/virtual-assistant-editorial-quality-checklist) for adjacent review steps, and [NIST small business cybersecurity guidance](https://www.nist.gov/itl/smallbusinesscyber) when the routine handles business information.

## Virtual Assistant Canonical URL Check for Article Publishing: operating steps

Record the slug before release and compare it with the route configuration, canonical tag, internal index entry, and sitemap location. Treat punctuation, pluralization, and word order as exact values.

## Inspect the rendered page

Load the canonical route directly and confirm a successful response, the intended title and body, and a self-referencing canonical URL. A fallback template that returns a successful status is still a failure.

## Look for accidental alternatives

Test likely draft and typo routes only when the release checklist identifies them. They should not render the same article as silent aliases. Record redirects separately if a deliberate migration has been approved.

## Verify discovery paths

Open the appropriate blog or research index and the generated sitemap. Confirm that both point to the same canonical route and that an older slug was not added by mistake.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a canonical tag enough?

No. The route, page content, internal links, and sitemap must agree; a tag cannot repair duplicate route behavior by itself.

### When may a slug change?

Only through an approved migration that accounts for redirects, links, sitemap updates, and historical traffic.
