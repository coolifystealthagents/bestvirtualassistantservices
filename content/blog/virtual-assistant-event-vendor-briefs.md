# Virtual Assistant Event Vendor Briefs

Event vendors need a shared understanding of deliverables and timing. A concise brief gives a virtual assistant a dependable reference while preserving the owner’s authority over commitments and changes.

## Virtual Assistant Event Vendor Briefs: state the outcome

Describe the event need, venue or delivery context, service boundary, and important dates. Separate confirmed details from preferred options.

## Track dependencies

List items such as access windows, guest counts, artwork, or approvals that affect the vendor’s work. Assign an owner and review date to each open dependency.

## Keep conversations organized

Record the vendor contact role, latest exchange, requested response, and source link. The [event operations guide](/blog/virtual-assistant-event-operations) supports coordination, while the [vendor onboarding guide](/blog/virtual-assistant-vendor-onboarding) helps structure first contact.

## Escalate commitments

Surface changes to scope, timing, safety, or contractual terms before communicating acceptance. Preparation and coordination are different from authorizing a commitment.

## Protect event information

Share only the details required for the vendor’s work. For access and information handling, review [NIST small business cybersecurity guidance](https://www.nist.gov/itl/smallbusinesscyber).
