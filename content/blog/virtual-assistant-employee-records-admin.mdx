# Virtual Assistant Employee Records Administration

Administrative support for employee records requires a clear handoff point. A virtual assistant can sort incoming material, check whether a packet appears complete, and maintain an audit trail. Authorized HR or legal owners define policy and make decisions about access, corrections, retention, and disputed records.

## Virtual Assistant Employee Records Administration follows a lifecycle

Use a simple lifecycle map. The employer may use different labels, but the handoff logic should remain visible:

`intake -> classification -> completeness check -> authorized storage -> controlled update -> review or handoff -> retention action`

The map describes movement, not permission to make policy. At every stage, record the current owner and the next action. If a record does not fit the map, pause it for the responsible owner instead of creating a new rule in the moment.

For a practical example of separating document facts from a reviewer’s decision, see the [document review process](/blog/virtual-assistant-document-review-process). Employee records require tighter access boundaries, but the administrative distinction still matters: identify what is present before asking an authorized owner what it means.

## Intake: capture context before filing

When a document arrives, record where it came from, the employee reference used by the organization, the document type, the received date, and the person responsible for review. Do not copy private details into a general tracking sheet when a reference number will do.

An email attachment is not automatically the authoritative record. Note the attachment’s location and ask whether it belongs in the employee record, a case file, or a temporary review area. This small distinction prevents a convenient first copy from becoming the source of truth by accident.

## Completeness checks are administrative checks

A completeness check asks whether the expected item is present, readable, associated with the right employee reference, and ready for the designated reviewer. It does not decide whether the contents satisfy a legal or employment requirement.

Use the employer's approved list of expected documents. Mark an item as present, missing, unclear, or awaiting owner review. Do not infer that a blank field is acceptable, and do not fill a missing value from memory. A short note such as "signature page not located" is more useful than a confident but unsupported conclusion.

## Access follows assigned responsibility

Keep access limited to the people who need the record for their assigned work. Separate ordinary administrative material from categories the employer has designated as restricted. Check the approved storage location before uploading a file, and do not forward employee material to a personal account for convenience.

If a shared link, folder, or role change makes access unclear, stop and ask the designated owner. Job titles alone do not establish permission. The owner can confirm whether access should be granted, removed, or reviewed by someone else.

## Correction requests need a visible trail

When someone says a record is wrong, preserve the request and identify what needs review. Do not silently overwrite the existing document. Record the date of the request, the affected record, the person who must decide, and the current status.

The authorized owner may instruct the assistant to retain an earlier version, replace a file, add a note, or route the matter elsewhere. Follow that instruction exactly. A correction log should describe the action without reproducing unnecessary personal information.

## Retention instructions come from the employer

A virtual assistant should apply a documented retention instruction, not invent one. Record the policy reference or owner instruction used for the action. If the schedule is missing, contradictory, or difficult to interpret, pause the deletion or transfer and request a decision.

The same rule applies to legal holds, employee requests, and questions about access history. Route them to the authorized HR or legal owner. The [U.S. Department of Labor records page](https://www.dol.gov/general/aboutdol/foia/records) offers general context about records access, but it does not replace the employer’s policy or legal advice.

## A missing signature and two versions

Suppose a manager sends a personnel form and says the signed page was included. The stored packet contains an unsigned copy. Later, an employee sends a version with different wording and a signature. The assistant should not choose the version that looks more complete.

First, preserve both files in the approved review location and record their source and arrival dates. Second, mark the signed page as present in the later submission but leave the document identity unresolved. Third, notify the authorized HR owner that the packets conflict. Include links to the files if the recipient has permission, not the private contents in an open channel.

The HR owner decides which version governs, whether the earlier copy must remain, and whether anyone else should review the discrepancy. The assistant then records that decision, applies the instructed filing action, and closes the open item with a date and owner. The important work is traceability, not guessing which scan "looks official."

## Role changes require a deliberate handoff

When an employee changes role, update the administrative record only after the authorized owner confirms the effective date and the fields that may change. Do not propagate a role change into every connected system from an informal message unless the workflow explicitly permits it.

List the systems or folders that require review, the person responsible for each, and any access change awaiting approval. Keep the old assignment visible when the record’s history requires it. A handoff note should show what was completed, what remains open, and who owns the next decision.

## Offboarding is a handoff, not a deletion shortcut

At offboarding, follow the employer’s documented sequence for account access, record ownership, open requests, and retention. Identify records that need a new owner and tasks that cannot close until HR or legal confirms the next step. Do not delete employee material because a user account is being closed.

Before the handoff ends, reconcile the tracker with the approved storage locations. Record unresolved items separately. Remove access only when the authorized owner or policy directs it, and keep evidence of that instruction.

An [inbox delegation guide](/blog/virtual-assistant-inbox-delegation-rules) can help explain why a handoff needs an explicit owner and stopping point. Employee records add privacy and employment context, so use the employer’s own rules for the final decision.

## Keep the administrative boundary clear

Good records administration is careful, limited, and easy to audit. The assistant keeps sources identifiable, checks agreed requirements, protects access, and escalates uncertainty. HR and legal owners decide policy, interpretation, and exceptions. That boundary lets administrative work move forward without turning a filing task into an unapproved employment decision.
