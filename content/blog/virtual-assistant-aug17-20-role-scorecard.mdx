# Virtual assistant role scorecards

Name recurring outcomes, tools, working window, quality checks, owner decisions, and escalation conditions. This guide is for buyers and operators who need a clear role boundary before assigning work to a Philippines-based virtual assistant.

## Virtual assistant role scorecards starts with a clear brief

Test the actual work a Philippines-based virtual assistant will perform and separate must-have tasks from later additions. Put the expected output, source of truth, review owner, and stop condition in the brief. If the request changes, record the change instead of quietly expanding the role.

## Keep preparation separate from decisions

Update the scorecard when the owner keeps clarifying the same requirement. The virtual assistant can gather context, normalize records, flag missing evidence, and prepare a review queue. The accountable owner keeps decisions involving commitments, sensitive information, public statements, or irreversible changes.

## Make the handoff easy to inspect

At handoff, include completed work, unresolved questions, source links, next owner, and review date. Use the [service role library](/services) to compare adjacent support lanes and the [provider vetting checklist](/provider-vetting) to ask about screening, supervision, access, and backup. For a security baseline, consult the [NIST small business cybersecurity guidance](https://www.nist.gov/itl/smallbusinesscyber).

## Frequently asked questions

### What should the owner define first?

Define the intended outcome, source of truth, reviewer, and approval boundary before assigning the first task.

### When should the virtual assistant escalate?

Escalate when information is missing, the request leaves the brief, a sensitive decision is involved, or the expected result cannot be checked.

### What makes a handoff ready?

A new reviewer can understand status, evidence, open questions, and next action without reconstructing a private conversation.
