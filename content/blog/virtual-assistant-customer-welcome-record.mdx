# A Customer Onboarding Checklist That Supports a Warm Handoff

A customer onboarding checklist keeps welcome details, access questions, promised materials, and follow-up ownership visible during the first stage of a relationship.

## A Customer Onboarding Checklist That Supports a Warm Handoff begins with the right facts

List the customer’s stated goal, agreed starting point, contact preference, required access, and next milestone. Separate completed welcome steps from items waiting on the customer or owner. Use the [work intake guide](/blog/virtual-assistant-client-onboarding-controls) to state the requested outcome, relevant deadline, and decision owner before details are gathered. A focused brief keeps support work useful without asking for information that does not affect the next step.

## Check context before changing a record

Compare the current record with the approved source and note the date checked. Keep original wording when it carries meaning, and distinguish a confirmed fact from a suggestion or interpretation. If two sources disagree, preserve both references and make the conflict visible.

## Keep authority and uncertainty visible

Do not promise a service outcome or alter account permissions without explicit authority. Escalate sensitive access questions and any mismatch between the welcome message and the approved terms. A virtual assistant can prepare information, organize a review list, and point out missing evidence; the owner or designated specialist should make decisions that change commitments, public statements, access, or sensitive records.

## Make the handoff easy to review

Close the handoff with a dated status, open questions, and the next human touchpoint. A clear owner for each open item prevents a warm welcome from becoming an untracked promise. A [document management guide](/blog/virtual-assistant-project-status-reporting) can keep the approved source separate from working notes. For general security considerations when handling business records, consult the [NIST small business cybersecurity guidance](https://www.nist.gov/itl/smallbusinesscyber).

## Questions worth answering before the work is closed

### What is the source of truth?

Name the record or approved document that controls the decision, and record when it was last checked.

### What belongs in an exception note?

State the missing or conflicting fact, the evidence reviewed, the risk of guessing, and who should decide.

### When is the handoff ready?

It is ready when completed items, unresolved questions, source links, and the next decision owner are all clear.
