# Virtual Assistant Customer Escalation Notes

For customer escalation notes, begin with the record that makes the next decision possible, then keep the owner and stopping point visible.

## Virtual Assistant Customer Escalation Notes: Capture the customer’s issue in their terms

An escalation note should open with what the customer says happened, not a conclusion about who is at fault. Add the date, channel, account or order reference, and the requested outcome. Neutral phrasing helps the next reviewer understand the concern without making the customer repeat it.

## Separate facts from interpretation

Use distinct labels for verified facts, the customer’s account, and an internal hypothesis. Link each fact to the record that supports it. If something cannot be checked, say so. This prevents a confident guess from hardening into the official version as the issue moves between people.

## Describe impact and urgency

Explain what the customer cannot do now, what deadline is approaching, and whether anyone faces a safety, privacy, or financial concern. Urgency should reflect impact and a real time constraint, not the volume of a message. A simple impact statement helps the responsible owner choose the right path.

## Name the promised next step

Every escalation should identify who will respond, by when, and through which channel. If no promise has been made, do not invent one; state the next internal check instead. Keep the customer-facing commitment separate from an internal aspiration so expectations remain accurate.

## Close with an accountable outcome

When resolved, add the decision, the evidence used, and the message sent to the customer. If the issue remains open, carry forward the unanswered question and owner. The [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau complaint guidance](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-complaint-en-1443/) illustrates why a clear account of the problem and response matters.


## Keep the decision visible

For this topic, preserve the source and route uncertainty to the named owner. Reference: [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-complaint-en-1443/).
