# Keep meeting notes useful after the VA changes

## How to approach: Keep meeting notes useful after the VA changes

Meeting notes are useful when they preserve decisions, owners, and dates rather than a transcript of the conversation. A new assistant should be able to open the notes and know what was decided and what happens next. If the notes only summarize the discussion, the work will be reconstructed from memory. Review the first few notes together before the process expands.

Choose a format with four fields: decision, owner, due date, and open question. Add a short context line at the top so a reader who missed the meeting understands the purpose. Keep the format the same across meetings so the notes are easy to scan. Keep the evidence with the record rather than in a separate log.

## Record decisions, not opinions

Write what was decided in a sentence that can be tested. A note that says the team discussed the timeline is not a decision. A note that says the timeline moves to the fifteenth and the client will be told by Friday is a decision. Record the observation time so the next reviewer can tell when the evidence was checked.

If no decision was made, say so and name the next meeting or the person who will decide. An ambiguous note invites a new assistant to assume a decision that never happened. A control that depends on memory is not yet a control. Record the exception and the person who approved it.

## Name the owner and the date

Every action needs one owner and one due date. A team name is not an owner. A date without a year or time zone is not a date. Record the owner in the same line as the action so the note can be scanned.

If an action depends on someone outside the meeting, record the dependency. A new assistant should know whether the action is waiting on a client, a manager, or a system change. Keep the evidence with the record rather than in a separate log. Update the template when the same question appears twice.

## Keep the open questions visible

Open questions are often more useful than decisions because they show where the work could stall. Write the question, the person who can answer it, and the date the answer is needed. Do not bury the question in a paragraph. Ask whether a new reviewer could act on the record without a conversation.

Review the open questions at the start of the next meeting. A question that has been open for weeks is a signal that the owner or the scope is wrong. A short note with four fields is easier to use than a long narrative. Name the owner in the same line as the action.

## Make the notes findable

Store the notes in the same place with a consistent title and date. A new assistant should not have to search three tools to find the last decision. Link the notes to the task or client record when the convention allows it. Separate what was observed from what was assumed.

Keep the raw transcript separate from the summary. The summary is the working record, while the transcript is the archive. A reader should not have to read the transcript to find the decision. Test the rule on one real task before applying it to the queue.

## Review the notes at handover

When the assistant changes, read the last few notes together. Ask the new assistant to explain the open decisions and actions. If the notes do not support that explanation, improve the format before the next meeting. Write the deadline as a date and a local time.

A good note format reduces the time a new assistant needs to become useful. It also protects the team when the person who took the notes is unavailable. Ask whether a new reviewer could act on the record without a conversation. Keep the evidence with the record rather than in a separate log.
