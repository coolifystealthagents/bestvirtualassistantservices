# Virtual Assistant Vendor Invoice Checklists

For vendor invoice checklists, begin with the record that makes the next decision possible, then keep the owner and stopping point visible.

## Virtual Assistant Vendor Invoice Checklists: Match the invoice to the agreement

Check vendor identity, invoice number, service period, purchase order or engagement reference, tax treatment fields, and payment instructions against the approved record. A familiar logo is not evidence that an invoice belongs to the current agreement.

## Verify the delivered work

Ask the responsible owner to confirm what was received, when, and whether it met the agreed scope. Keep that approval separate from data entry. If a line is unclear, request clarification instead of converting an ambiguous description into an assumption.

## Watch for changed payment details

A new bank account or urgent request deserves independent confirmation through a known vendor contact. Do not validate payment changes using the phone number or link in the unexpected message. This simple pause prevents a clerical task from becoming a security incident.

## Record exceptions without blocking facts

Classify missing tax detail, duplicate invoice, disputed line, pricing discrepancy, or approval gap. Pay what is properly approved according to policy while routing the unresolved issue to its owner. Avoid editing the invoice to make it fit.

## Keep a complete trail

Store the invoice, approval, correspondence, and payment reference together under the organization’s retention rule. The [IRS independent contractor guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/independent-contractor-defined) is a helpful reminder that vendor classification and documentation can have tax significance.


## Keep the decision visible

For this topic, preserve the source and route uncertainty to the named owner.
