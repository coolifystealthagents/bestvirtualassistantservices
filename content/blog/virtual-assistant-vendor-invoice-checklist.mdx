One final review of vendor invoice checklists should ask whether the record tells the truth about its state. It should distinguish a verified fact from a proposed correction, a completed action from a waiting action, and a normal case from an exception. Those distinctions are especially important when an invoice matches an amount but belongs to the wrong period.
If the work is handed between people, preserve the source and the question together. A later reviewer should not have to reconstruct why a field was accepted, why a correction was declined, or why an item remained open. This simple record of reasoning protects continuity and makes the service easier to improve.
