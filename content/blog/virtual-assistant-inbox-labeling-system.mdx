One final review of inbox labeling systems should ask whether the record tells the truth about its state. It should distinguish a verified fact from a proposed correction, a completed action from a waiting action, and a normal case from an exception. Those distinctions are especially important when a message is labeled complete while a reply is still owed.
If the work is handed between people, preserve the source and the question together. A later reviewer should not have to reconstruct why a field was accepted, why a correction was declined, or why an item remained open. This simple record of reasoning protects continuity and makes the service easier to improve.
