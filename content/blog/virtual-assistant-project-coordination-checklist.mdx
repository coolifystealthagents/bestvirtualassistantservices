# Virtual Assistant Project Coordination Checklist

A dependable assistant workflow begins with a narrow task lane, clear examples, and a named reviewer. The goal is to make recurring work easier to assign, check, and improve.

## Virtual Assistant Project Coordination Checklist starts with a written brief

Write the input, expected output, tool, timing, definition of done, and stop condition. Include two or three examples of acceptable work and one example that needs revision. A concise brief gives the assistant a source of truth and gives the manager a fair basis for review. Use the [service role library](/services) to compare adjacent role lanes before combining unrelated work.

## Set review and escalation rules

Decide what the assistant can complete, what needs a second look, and what must remain owner-only. Review early work on a predictable rhythm, record recurring questions, and update the brief when the same confusion appears twice. The [provider vetting checklist](/provider-vetting) can help you ask how screening, training, backup, and quality review are handled.

## Use evidence and protect the workflow

Keep records in approved systems, use named accounts where possible, and document exceptions instead of relying on memory. The [NIST small business cybersecurity guidance](https://www.nist.gov/itl/smallbusinesscyber) provides a useful baseline for access, passwords, updates, and protecting business information. Do not ask the assistant to make legal, medical, financial, refund, or sensitive customer decisions without explicit approval.

## Build a useful handoff

A good handoff states what was completed, what is waiting, what changed, what needs approval, and what should happen next. Keep the format short enough to use every day. After the workflow is stable, measure manager review time, correction patterns, missed deadlines, and escalation quality before adding another task lane.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should a new assistant do first?

Start with one repeatable workflow and a small set of safe examples. Expand only after accuracy and review become predictable.

### How often should the work be reviewed?

Review frequency should match risk. Daily review is sensible during onboarding, while stable low-risk work may move to a weekly scorecard.

### When is the workflow ready to scale?

Scale when the instructions are current, access is controlled, work is accurate, exceptions are visible, and the manager spends less time repairing the handoff.
