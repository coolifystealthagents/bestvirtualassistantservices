# Remote Work Continuity Planning for Virtual Assistants

Reliable work continuity work depends on making routine decisions visible. This research brief translates authoritative guidance into an operating pattern a virtual assistant can use, with a reviewer responsible for consequential judgment. Link the finished workflow to the [research library](/research) and the [service overview](/services) for context.

## Remote Work Continuity Planning: define the control

Start with a named owner, a clear input, an expected output, and a stop condition. Record the date, source scope, and exceptions before work begins. The assistant should prepare evidence and options, while the client or subject-matter owner approves policy, access, and material changes.

## Evidence and workflow design

Use a small register with these fields: item, source, date, status, reviewer, and next action. Separate direct observations from interpretation. NIST and CISA guidance support explicit controls and risk-based prioritization [1][2]. Records guidance also makes retention and retrieval responsibilities easier to audit [5].

| Control point | Assistant action | Reviewer check | Stop condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Intake | Capture request, owner, scope, and due date | Confirm priority and authority | Missing decision owner |
| Preparation | Gather source-backed facts and open questions | Check dates, scope, and denominator | Evidence cannot be reproduced |
| Handoff | Summarize decision options and unresolved risk | Approve, revise, or reject | Material uncertainty remains |
| Review | Log outcome and next review date | Confirm the record is complete | Exception is unassigned |

## Measures that matter

Track completion time, rework count, exception age, and the share of items with a named owner. These measures are operational signals, not universal benchmarks. Use a consistent definition and preserve the denominator so a future reviewer can compare like with like.

A useful first dashboard has three views: work in progress, overdue exceptions, and completed items awaiting review. Avoid combining unlike units into one score. The [project status reporting guide](/research/virtual-assistant-project-status-reporting) offers a related handoff pattern.

## Privacy, access, and inclusion

Apply least privilege, use approved accounts, and avoid copying confidential customer data into unapproved tools. When the workflow includes a customer-facing document or meeting, check accessibility early against W3C guidance [4]. Security and privacy decisions belong to the authorized owner, not to an assistant acting alone.

## Methodology and limitations

This brief reviewed ten official standards and guidance sources on 2026-08-10 and synthesized recurring control patterns for work continuity. It is an operational starting point, not legal, security, continuity, or accessibility advice for a specific organization. Validate local requirements and source versions before adoption.

## Key takeaways

- Name the owner, evidence boundary, and stop condition before execution.
- Preserve dates, scope, units, and exceptions through every handoff.
- Measure rework and exception age instead of rewarding unchecked speed.
- Escalate access, privacy, safety, and policy decisions to the authorized reviewer.

## FAQs

### What should a virtual assistant decide independently?

Routine preparation and documentation can be delegated when the boundary is explicit. Decisions involving access, legal obligations, customer harm, or policy should be escalated.

### Which metric should be tracked first?

Start with completion time plus rework count. Together they show throughput and whether apparent speed is creating avoidable correction work.

### How often should the workflow be reviewed?

Set a review date based on risk and change frequency. Review sooner after an incident, a system change, or a material change in the underlying guidance.
