# Virtual Assistant SOP Maintenance Controls: A Research Brief

An SOP is useful only when a person can find the current version and trust its scope. This brief turns maintenance into a repeatable control.

## Define the control owner

Record the process owner, reviewer, source systems, review interval, and retirement rule. The [document control brief](/research/virtual-assistant-document-control) provides a companion record structure.

## Use three gates

Draft changes need evidence, an owner review, and a dated release. The [knowledge-base maintenance brief](/research/virtual-assistant-knowledge-base-maintenance) covers retrieval checks. NIST and Archives guidance support access, retention, and accountability controls.

| Gate | Assistant check | Owner decision | Stop condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Evidence | Link the triggering change | Accept or reject | No reliable source |
| Review | Compare steps and permissions | Approve scope | Safety ambiguity |
| Release | Update version and index | Publish current copy | Old copy still discoverable |

## Methodology and limitations

We reviewed ten official standards, security, records, accessibility, privacy, and work-practice sources on 2026-08-10. The result is an operational control model, not legal or compliance advice.

## Key takeaways

- Every SOP needs one accountable owner.
- Version and retirement metadata prevent silent divergence.
- A retrieval test is part of publishing, not an optional cleanup.

## FAQs

### How often should SOPs be reviewed?

Use a risk-based interval and review immediately after a material system, policy, or workflow change.

### Can an assistant approve an SOP?

An assistant can check completeness and evidence. The process owner should approve risk, permissions, and exceptions.
