# Accessibility Review Controls for Virtual Assistant Teams

Accessibility Review works best when the queue, evidence trail, and decision owner are visible. This brief covers intake, preparation, review, exception routing, and handoff without turning administrative support into unapproved judgment. It is written for small businesses building proper routines for daily article creation and related operations.

## Define the accessibility review routine

Write the audience, trigger, required inputs, expected output, due time, source of truth, and exception owner. A narrow queue makes quality review possible. The [website content updates](/research/virtual-assistant-website-content-updates) and [seo quality control](/research/virtual-assistant-seo-quality-control) place this routine in the wider evidence workflow.

## Build an operating brief

Record required fields, examples of an acceptable result, the local and client time zones, and the review cadence. The assistant may complete documented steps and flag records that cannot be verified. The assistant should not infer sensitive traits, invent missing data, or make consequential decisions.

## Use a safe work test

Give every finalist the same paid exercise with dummy records. Include a normal request, a missing fact, a conflicting source, a sensitive request, and a case outside written authority. Score accuracy, source use, notes, questions, escalation, and completion time.

## Control access and records

Use a named account, multi-factor authentication, and least privilege. Keep recovery and administrator powers with the client. Record approvals, source dates, version status, and offboarding actions in the approved system. Privacy and security frameworks provide control baselines, not a substitute for the client policy.

## Make exceptions easy to route

An exception record should show the item, facts checked, missing decision, risk, owner, and next review time. Do not hide uncertainty to make the queue look complete. Unexpected credential requests or suspicious links should be reported through the client path.

## Review the first month

During days 1 through 3, use examples and review every output. During days 4 through 10, allow only the narrow queue and label defects by type. During days 11 through 30, compare accuracy, exception quality, manager time, and access behavior before expanding scope.

## Handoff across time zones

End each shift with completed items, blocked items, decisions needed, and the first next action. Use descriptive headings, link text, and table labels so the handoff is accessible and easy to audit.

## Control table

| Stage | Assistant action | Owner review | Stop condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Intake | Confirm source, scope, and required fields | Check priority and authority | Inputs are missing |
| Prepare | Complete documented steps and capture evidence | Sample the result | Result cannot be verified |
| Exception | Record facts and route the decision | Decide the exception | Sensitive or irreversible action |
| Handoff | List complete, blocked, and next items | Resolve open decisions | Owner is unavailable |
| Offboarding | Return work and open items | Suspend access and preserve records | Account or asset is untracked |

## Methodology and limitations

This brief synthesizes the ten direct sources listed below, reviewed on 2026-08-09. It separates administrative workflow guidance from legal, security, employment, financial, medical, and other professional judgments. The sources support repeatable controls; they do not guarantee candidate performance or a business outcome.

## Key takeaways

- Define the queue and owner decision before live work.
- Preserve dates, sources, versions, and uncertainty through every handoff.
- Expand access only after the original routine is stable and reviewable.

## FAQs

### What can the assistant own?

The assistant can complete documented administrative steps and flag items that cannot be verified. The client retains consequential decisions and final release authority.

### How should the work be tested?

Use the same dummy-data exercise and scorecard for every candidate. Reward accurate escalation when a request falls outside the written scope.

### What access is appropriate?

Use a named account, multi-factor authentication, and only the permissions needed for the first task set. Review access before expanding the queue.

### What should be reviewed each week?

Sample completed work, inspect exceptions, check stale items, compare manager correction time, and confirm that no access or policy boundary drifted.

### When should scope expand?

Expand only after the owner sees stable quality, clear evidence, useful handoffs, and no unexplained access or policy exceptions.
