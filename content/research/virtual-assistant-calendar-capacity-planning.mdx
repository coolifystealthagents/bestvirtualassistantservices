# Virtual Assistant Calendar Capacity Planning: A Research Brief

Capacity planning protects quality by making available working time, coordination time, and uncertainty visible before commitments are made.

## Establish the planning baseline

Record working windows, recurring commitments, time zones, service levels, and planned buffers. The [calendar rules brief](/research/virtual-assistant-calendar-management-rules) covers permission boundaries.

## Review capacity weekly

Compare planned work with actual effort and open exceptions. The [weekly operations report](/research/virtual-assistant-weekly-operations-report) provides a handoff format.

| Input | Planning question | Escalation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Availability | What time is genuinely open? | Conflicting commitments |
| Demand | Which work is due and why? | Unowned priority |
| Buffer | What absorbs interruption? | Buffer consumed |

## Methodology and limitations

Ten official sources on remote work, accessibility, security, privacy, and records were reviewed on 2026-08-10. Actual capacity depends on local contracts, workload, and approved policies.

## Key takeaways

- Capacity is available time after known commitments, not the whole calendar.
- Buffers should be visible and protected.
- Conflicts need an owner decision rather than silent overbooking.

## FAQs

### Should buffers be assigned as tasks?

They can be reserved as protected capacity so urgent work has a visible tradeoff.

### Who resolves a priority conflict?

The designated work owner should choose the tradeoff and record the decision.
