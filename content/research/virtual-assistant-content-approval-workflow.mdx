# Virtual Assistant Content Approval Workflow: A Research Brief

Content approval should prove what changed, why it changed, who reviewed it, and which evidence supports the final claim.

## Start with a complete brief

Record audience, intent, sources, internal links, accessibility requirements, owner, and publish date. The [SEO quality-control brief](/research/virtual-assistant-seo-quality-control) adds release checks.

## Review in four passes

Check factual support, clarity, accessibility, and metadata. The [website update brief](/research/virtual-assistant-website-content-updates) helps separate approved edits from unrequested changes.

| Pass | Evidence | Stop condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Facts | Source notes | Claim unsupported |
| Audience | Intent and scope | Promise unclear |
| Access | Headings, links, alt text | Barrier found |
| Release | Owner and version | Approval missing |

## Methodology and limitations

Ten official sources covering accessibility, security, privacy, records, and work practices were reviewed on 2026-08-10. Editorial judgment and organization-specific policy remain necessary.

## Key takeaways

- A brief is the contract for the requested content change.
- Approval should cover facts, accessibility, and release metadata.
- Preserve a versioned record of the evidence and decision.

## FAQs

### Can an assistant publish without review?

Only under an explicit, risk-appropriate delegation. Otherwise route the final artifact to the publishing owner.

### What happens when a source conflicts?

Pause the claim, document the conflict, and ask the owner to resolve or narrow it.
