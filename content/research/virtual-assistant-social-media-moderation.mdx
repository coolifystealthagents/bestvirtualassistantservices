# Social Media Moderation by Virtual Assistants: Research and Control Brief

Moderation is a queue-management job until a post involves safety, legal risk, a security incident, or a reputation decision. Those cases need a named owner and a clear stop condition.

## Classify the queue before replying

Use labels such as routine question, approved service request, complaint, safety concern, and suspected account compromise. The [provider vetting guide](/provider-vetting) can score whether a candidate recognizes when to stop.

The [customer inbox triage brief](/research/virtual-assistant-customer-inbox-triage) provides a related queue model.

## Response rules need examples

Provide approved language, prohibited claims, response windows, escalation contacts, and a record format. Never ask an assistant to improvise a crisis response or disclose private customer information. FTC and NIST privacy guidance provide useful control questions [4][5].

## Moderation decision table

| Queue label | Assistant action | Owner action | Stop condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Routine | Use approved response | Sample review | Missing context |
| Complaint | Acknowledge and route | Decide remedy | Threat or legal claim |
| Safety | Preserve facts and escalate | Handle incident | Immediate danger |
| Security | Do not click or forward | Activate response plan | Credential request |

## Make records auditable

Capture timestamp, platform, message ID, label, action, approved template, escalation, and next review. Records guidance supports retaining enough context to explain what happened without creating uncontrolled duplicates [10].

## Design for accessible communication

Clear labels, plain language, descriptive links, and captions help both moderators and audiences. Use WCAG as a baseline, then test the actual platform [3].

## Methodology and limitations

This brief synthesizes official security, privacy, accessibility, copyright, records, and remote-work resources reviewed on 2026-08-07. Platform policies and local laws still require specialist review.

## Key takeaways

- Triage is not the same as making a policy or crisis decision.
- The safest response to uncertainty is a visible escalation.
- Review samples and exceptions, not only response volume.

## FAQs

### Can an assistant delete comments?

Only when the owner has documented rules, the action is reversible or logged, and the content clearly meets the rule. Otherwise, preserve context and escalate.

### What is a useful quality measure?

Track correct classification, approved-response use, escalation accuracy, missed risks, and time to owner review.
