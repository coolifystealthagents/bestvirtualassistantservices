# Research Briefs for Virtual Assistants: Evidence Control and Handoffs

Research support is valuable when another person can reproduce the path from claim to source. This routine helps a virtual assistant prepare evidence while keeping interpretation with the client.

## Start with a research question

Write the audience, decision, date boundary, inclusion rules, and expected output. A focused question reduces copying and makes gaps visible. Link the final brief to the [research library](/research) so the team can compare clusters.

## Build a source register

For each source, record publisher, title, URL, publication date, access date, relevant passage, claim supported, and confidence. Label each note as direct fact, reported finding, or proposed interpretation. The [contact page](/contact-us) should never be used to send unreviewed research or confidential source material.

## Evidence table

| Evidence type | Assistant action | Reviewer action | Stop condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Direct fact | Quote or paraphrase with location | Confirm wording and date | Source is inaccessible |
| Reported finding | Preserve study context | Check population and method | Context is missing |
| Interpretation | Mark as a proposal | Decide whether it belongs | Judgment is presented as fact |

## Verify before summarizing

Compare the source’s scope, unit, date, and denominator. Do not combine unlike measures into a score. OECD and NIST publishing resources reinforce the importance of provenance and clear standards [1][10].

## Handoffs should expose uncertainty

End with accepted claims, open questions, rejected sources, and the next owner action. A reviewer should be able to find the evidence without asking the assistant to reconstruct the entire search.

## Security, privacy, and copyright boundaries

Use approved accounts and minimum access. Do not paste private customer information into an unapproved research tool. Copyright and privacy guidance are guardrails for handling excerpts and personal data [5][7].

## Methodology and limitations

This is a practical research-control brief based on official standards and guidance reviewed on 2026-08-07. It does not replace source-specific interpretation, legal advice, or the client’s information-security policy.

## Key takeaways

- A source register is part of the deliverable, not an optional appendix.
- Date, population, denominator, and scope must survive every handoff.
- When evidence conflicts, record the conflict and escalate it.

## FAQs

### Should the assistant choose the conclusion?

The assistant can present supported alternatives and explain the evidence trail. The client or subject-matter owner should make the consequential judgment.

### How many sources are enough?

Enough depends on the question. Start with authoritative sources, then add independent corroboration until material claims are defensible and remaining gaps are explicit.
