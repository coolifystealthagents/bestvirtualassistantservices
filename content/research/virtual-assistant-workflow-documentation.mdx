# Workflow Documentation for Virtual Assistants

Good workflow documentation work makes context, ownership, and review visible. This research brief turns authoritative guidance into a practical operating pattern for a virtual assistant. Link the finished workflow to the [research library](/research) and [service overview](/services).

## Workflow Documentation: define the control

Record the request, owner, scope, due date, source boundary, and stop condition. The assistant can prepare evidence, drafts, and exception notes. An authorized owner should approve policy, access, customer impact, and other consequential decisions.

## Evidence and workflow design

Maintain a register with item, source, date, status, reviewer, and next action. Keep direct observations separate from interpretation. Risk and security guidance supports explicit controls and accountable review [1][2][3]. Records guidance supports durable retrieval and disposition decisions [5].

| Control point | Assistant action | Reviewer check | Stop condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Intake | Capture scope, owner, and priority | Confirm authority | Owner is missing |
| Preparation | Gather facts and open questions | Check dates and context | Evidence is not reproducible |
| Handoff | Summarize options and risk | Approve or revise | Material uncertainty remains |
| Review | Log outcome and next date | Confirm completeness | Exception is unassigned |

## Measures that matter

Track completion time, correction count, exception age, and the percentage of items with a named owner. These are local operating measures, not universal benchmarks. Preserve definitions and denominators so comparisons remain meaningful. The [project status reporting guide](/research/virtual-assistant-project-status-reporting) provides a related handoff pattern.

## Privacy, access, and inclusion

Use approved accounts and minimum necessary access. Do not copy confidential information into an unapproved tool. Check customer-facing outputs against W3C accessibility guidance [4], and escalate privacy, security, and policy decisions to the responsible owner.

## Methodology and limitations

This brief reviewed ten official standards and guidance sources on 2026-08-10 and synthesized recurring controls for workflow documentation. It is not legal, security, continuity, or accessibility advice for a specific organization. Confirm local requirements and current source versions before adoption.

## Key takeaways

- Define the owner, evidence boundary, and stop condition first.
- Preserve dates, scope, and unresolved questions across handoffs.
- Track correction and exception patterns, not unchecked speed.
- Escalate consequential decisions to an authorized reviewer.

## FAQs

### What can the assistant own?

It can own the defined preparation, documentation, and routine checks. The boundary must identify what requires review.

### What is the first useful metric?

Use completion time alongside correction count. Together they show throughput and whether speed creates rework.

### When should the workflow be reviewed?

Set a regular review date and review sooner after an incident, tool change, or material policy change.
