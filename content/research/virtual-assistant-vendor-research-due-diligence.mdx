# Vendor Research Due Diligence: A Virtual Assistant Buyer Research Brief

Vendor research is strongest when it helps an owner make a bounded decision. It is weakest when a polished claim is repeated as though it were independent evidence.

## Build an evidence register

NIST privacy and security frameworks encourage organizations to identify and govern risk [1][2]. For each vendor claim, record the exact statement, source type, date, scope, and what remains unverified. FTC advertising guidance is a useful reminder that marketing claims need substantiation [3].

## Compare evidence categories

| Category | Research question | Do not infer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capability | What work is described? | That every role includes it |
| Control | How is information handled? | That a policy proves practice |
| Fit | Which buyer need is addressed? | That a case study matches this case |
| Limitation | What is excluded or unknown? | That silence means approval |

Keep the [vendor renewal research](/research/virtual-assistant-vendor-renewal-tracking) separate from initial selection. Accessibility, copyright, and usability checks also affect the quality of public evidence [8][9][10]. The [services overview](/services) should remain a description of scope, not independent vendor evidence.

## Methodology and limitations

This review compares privacy, security, advertising, digital-policy, accessibility, copyright, and usability references checked on 2026-08-11. It is a research method, not a provider endorsement, legal opinion, or performance guarantee.

## Key takeaways

- Preserve the original wording of material claims.
- Compare the same evidence categories across vendors.
- Record uncertainty as a finding, not an inconvenience.
