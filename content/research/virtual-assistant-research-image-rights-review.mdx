# How Can a Virtual Assistant Review Image Rights Before a Research Article Uses a Hero Asset?

## Research question

This report asks how a virtual assistant can organize an image-rights check before a research page uses a hero asset without claiming legal clearance. A relevant image needs provenance as well as appeal. The assistant can collect origin, stated terms, access date, and visible risks. An owner or qualified adviser decides whether those facts are sufficient for use.

## A hero image needs provenance

Identify who created the asset, how it was obtained, what use is allowed, whether modification is permitted, and whether a person, logo, or private information creates another concern. “Looks generic” is not evidence of permission. The article’s image must not imply a testimonial, location, credential, or result the copy cannot support.

## Method: trace permission and intended use

Compare the planned site use with the stated permission. Preserve source or generation receipt, access date, restrictions, and modification status. If provenance is uncertain, stop the handoff and ask for a replacement or explicit owner decision. Keep the internal receipt separate from public copy.

## A rights-aware handoff

Record asset path, article route, descriptive alt text, origin, creator, permission statement, retrieval date, modification status, and unresolved question. Verify the asset is distinct from existing images and does not reuse an uncertain stock image or mark. The assistant checks fields and escalates gaps; it cannot grant a license.

## Limitations and conclusion

This review organizes evidence but is not legal advice or a guarantee of permission. Terms can be incomplete, changed, or jurisdiction-specific. The evidence supports a bounded conclusion: use a hero asset only when provenance and intended use are reviewable, and route uncertainty before publication.

## Visible meaning is part of the review

A rights check is incomplete if the image tells a story the article does not support. A staged desk scene should not imply a real client, a location, or a measured result. Alt text should describe the useful visual fact without adding an invented claim. The assistant can compare image meaning with article scope and flag a mismatch for the editor.

## Distinct assets and retained history

A new hero asset should have a unique route-specific identity and a retained receipt. Do not silently replace an existing article image or reuse an uncertain asset merely because it is convenient. Record the exact path and intended route, then check that the public page points to that asset. The manifest remains repository evidence and is not public copy.

## When to escalate

Escalate if permission language is absent or ambiguous, if a recognizable person or mark appears, if the source page is inaccessible, if the asset resembles a stock image with uncertain terms, or if the image would imply a claim. The assistant can identify these conditions and pause the handoff. It cannot waive them to meet a publishing target.

## Evidence scope and limitations

The method is a desk-research synthesis of ten public sources selected for institutional authority and relevance to source handling, privacy, records, accessibility, remote work, helpful content, digital systems, and copyright. NIST and CISA frame risk and safeguards; the FTC discusses privacy and security expectations; W3C supplies structural accessibility guidance; the National Archives contributes records context; the ILO addresses remote-work conditions; OECD describes the digital economy; Google describes people-first content; and the Library of Congress provides copyright context. These sources do not measure this company, certify a provider, or prove a market-wide statistic. They support controls and reasoning, not a fabricated case study.

## Facts, analysis, and owner decisions

Keep three kinds of sentences visibly separate. A fact is a claim directly supported by a source. Analysis explains why that fact may matter to a defined virtual assistant services decision. An owner decision chooses wording, priority, escalation, or publication status. A clean handoff can contain all three, but it should label their relationship. This prevents a recommendation from masquerading as an external fact and gives the editor a precise place to correct the work.

## Safe operating boundary

The assistant may search public material, capture source context, compare the material against a defined question, draft a traceable note, and route an exception. The assistant should not approve sensitive claims, infer confidential company information, expose private credentials, submit forms, promise pricing or results, or publish against an owner hold. These boundaries are part of quality because a fast answer that exceeds authority is not a reliable service artifact.

## Small-sample test

Before making the rule routine, run a small fixed sample containing ordinary cases and at least one incomplete, changed, or conflicting source. Have an authorized editor inspect the handoff without relying on the assistant’s memory. Measure missing fields, corrections, exceptions caught, exceptions missed, and owner rework. Expand only when the evidence shows that the control finds problems early and the role boundary remains clear.
