# Calendar Booking Integrity: Research on Virtual Assistant Scheduling Controls

Scheduling assistance is a coordination service, not permission to make assumptions about a person's availability, attendees, or meeting purpose.

## Availability is not the whole decision

Calendar documentation distinguishes sharing and access choices [1][2]. A reliable scheduling brief should also name time zone, meeting length, buffer, attendee consent, and the owner who handles a conflict. These details matter more than a promise of fast booking.

## Use a state model

| State | Evidence | Safe next action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Requested | Sender and intent are known | Clarify missing constraints |
| Proposed | Candidate time is visible | Wait for required confirmation |
| Booked | Event is on the source calendar | Send accurate details |
| Changed | An existing commitment is affected | Escalate to the owner |

Least-privilege and privacy principles support narrowing access to what the role needs [3][4]. Accessibility and clear error prevention also belong in the experience [5][6]. Compare with the [calendar delegation research](/research/virtual-assistant-calendar-delegation) for related control questions. See the [services overview](/services) before translating a control into role scope.

## Methodology and limitations

This brief reviews official calendar documentation alongside privacy, security, accessibility, usability, advertising, and copyright guidance checked on 2026-08-11. It does not evaluate a particular calendar provider or guarantee error-free scheduling.

## Key takeaways

- Treat access and consent as separate decisions.
- Keep the source calendar authoritative.
- Escalate conflicts instead of silently rewriting commitments.
