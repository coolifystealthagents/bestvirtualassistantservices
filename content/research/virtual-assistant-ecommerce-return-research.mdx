# Virtual Assistant Ecommerce Return Research: Where Support Needs Boundaries

Return support sits between policy, customer communication, inventory facts, and a merchant's decision. A support role needs a clear boundary between explaining and approving.

## Verify the policy before the reply

The FTC's mail-order guidance illustrates why fulfillment promises and delay communication need accurate handling [1]. Privacy and security guidance also make customer information a control question, not merely a convenience [2][3]. A virtual assistant can locate an order and summarize the stated policy, while an exception should remain with the authorized owner.

## Model the return as states

| State | Evidence | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Requested | Order and reason are identified | Do not promise an exception |
| Eligible | Current policy is matched | Confirm any merchant rule |
| In transit | Carrier or warehouse evidence exists | Avoid inventing receipt |
| Exception | Facts do not match policy | Escalate for approval |

Clear error messages and understandable options support better service [5][6]. Compare the [order escalation research](/research/virtual-assistant-ecommerce-order-escalation) for adjacent handoffs. The [services overview](/services) is the appropriate place to define the support role.

## Methodology and limitations

This brief reviews FTC consumer and privacy guidance alongside security, accessibility, usability, copyright, and digital-policy sources checked on 2026-08-11. Policies vary by seller and jurisdiction, so this is not legal advice.

## Key takeaways

- Read the current merchant policy before drafting a reply.
- Separate policy explanation from exception approval.
- Record only the customer data needed for the case.
